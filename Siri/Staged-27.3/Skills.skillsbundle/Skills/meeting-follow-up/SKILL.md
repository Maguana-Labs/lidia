---
name: meeting-follow-up
description: Follows up on a meeting recorded in LidIA, such as sending the recap or nudging someone about what they promised. Use when the user asks to follow up on, email the recap of, or send next steps from a meeting.
---
Work out which meeting first. If the user is looking at a meeting in LidIA, use that one. Otherwise use the find meetings tool with the person, company or project the user named, and pick the most recent match that has already happened. If several meetings fit equally well (two calls with the same person this week, for example), read the titles and dates back and ask which one.

Then look at the meeting's action items with the list action items tool, filtered to that meeting. They tell you who owes what. A follow-up from the user usually covers what was agreed and what each side does next; when the user only wants to chase someone, keep to that person's open items.

To write the email on the Mac, prefer the draft follow-up tool: it drafts the recap in LidIA from the meeting notes and opens it for the user to review. If the user wants a follow-up about one specific action item ("remind Mateo about the contract"), use the prepare next step tool on that item instead; it knows who the item is for. On the iPhone, where those tools don't exist, hand the meeting to Mail as below.

When the user wants the recap sent through another app ("email the recap of my Northwind meeting to Mateo"), you can hand the meeting itself to Mail or Messages: it carries a plain-text recap, and a formatted one for Mail. Find the recipient in Contacts by the name the user gave.

Never send anything without the user's explicit go-ahead. Show or read the draft, say who it goes to, and ask before sending. LidIA's own tools never send: they open a sheet in LidIA where the user chooses Open in Mail or Copy.

If the meeting's notes are withheld (LidIA says meeting content stays on the device), don't try to reconstruct them from the transcript or other meetings. Tell the user to open the meeting in LidIA, and offer to open it.
